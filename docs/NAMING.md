# Naming note: can the app launch as "Dinkie Dino"?

**This is not legal advice.** It is a research note for the project owner to bring to a trademark attorney. Short answer: do not launch under "Dinkie Dino", free or paid. Keep an original name such as "Pocket Rexie".

## The question

Could the app launch free as "Dinkie Dino" and later release a paid, expanded version under the same name?

## What was found

- A web search found no USPTO record for "Dinkie Dino". That proves little. Search tools and third-party trademark mirrors are incomplete and often lag the real register.
- The mark may be registered abroad (Japan is the likely place, since the toy is also sold as "RakuRaku Dinokun"), and a foreign registration would not show up in a US search.
- It may be unregistered. Unregistered marks that are used in commerce still carry common-law rights.
- The toy is known as Dinkie Dino or RakuRaku Dinokun. Fan sources do not agree on the manufacturer, and that has not been verified, so the owner of any rights is unclear. Unclear is not the same as nobody.
- The name is live in commerce. There is a seller site, dinkiedino.store, and originals are resold on Etsy. A collectors' market is exactly where confusion with an app of the same name would show up.

## Why the name is risky

1. **Common-law rights.** Not being registered does not make a name free to use. A rights holder who used the name first can object.
2. **Confusion is the test.** The question is whether people would think the app comes from, or is approved by, the maker of the toy. An app named after a toy line, aimed at people who remember the toy, invites that.
3. **Being free first gives no safe harbor.** Launching without charging does not protect the name. It only means the audience, the reviews and the habit of the name are built on something that may have to be given up.
4. **A forced rebrand costs the most late.** If a takedown or demand letter arrives after the app has an audience, the owner loses the name people know, the search ranking and the reviews tied to it.
5. **Charging later raises the stakes.** Once money is involved, a claim can ask for profits as well as an end to use, and the other side has more reason to act.
6. **Apple acts fast.** Apple can remove an app after an intellectual property complaint with little notice, and the developer then has to resolve it with the complainant. A pulled app loses its listing and rank while that happens.
7. **Renaming later is allowed but not free.** Apple lets a developer change an app's name in a new version. Ratings and reviews carry over only when the app stays in the same app record. A new app record or bundle starts at zero.

## Recommendation

- Keep an original name. "Pocket Rexie" or another name that is clearly your own.
- Do not use "Dinkie", "Dinkie Dino", "Tamagotchi", "Digimon" or any "-gotchi" term in the name, subtitle, keywords or screenshots. See `docs/APP_STORE.md`.
- If you want to nod to the history, use plain prose in the description such as "inspired by classic keychain dino pets", and only after counsel has reviewed the wording. Keep it out of the name, subtitle and keyword field.
- Search before committing: the USPTO trademark database (the current replacement for TESS), the EUIPO eSearch plus tool, and the WIPO Global Brand Database, which covers many national registers including Japan. Search for the exact name and for similar spellings and sounds.
- Consider filing your own application for the name you choose, once clearance looks good.
- Have a trademark attorney confirm the clearance search and the final name before release.

## The free-then-paid plan

The pet, its species names and its pixel art are original, so a free launch followed by a paid tier does not depend on anyone else's name or art. That is the clean version of this plan, and it works with an original app name.

The project today has no monetization, by the owner's requirement: no ads, no in-app purchases, no subscription and no energy gates. A paid tier later is a separate decision and would change the privacy label, the App Store notes and the promise made to free users. If it is ever taken up:

- Prefer one app with a one-time or subscription unlock through StoreKit. Use a new bundle or app record only if there is a real reason, because a new record loses ratings and reviews.
- Do not charge for features that free users already had without clear notice. Moving existing features behind a paywall is what draws bad reviews and, in some cases, store scrutiny.
- Update `docs/PRIVACY.md`, the privacy label and the App Store description before shipping any paid feature.
